# Mutu Behavioral UX

The purpose is to make Mutu emotionally engaging, motivating and habit-forming while protecting trust, agency and wellbeing. **This is not permission to create dark patterns.**

Translate "the seven deadly sins" into ethical human-motivation principles. Consider all seven lenses during every UX review, but **implement only the two or three that genuinely support the user's goal**. Never force all seven mechanisms into one interface.

## Mutu's engagement philosophy

Mutu should not retain users through meaningless screen time. Mutu retains users because: their needs become visible; asking feels safer; helping feels natural; reciprocity creates belonging; completed exchanges create earned progress; relationships become stronger and easier to maintain; the product remembers how people met; returning to Mutu helps users take a useful next action.

The core engagement loop:

```text
Relevant opportunity
→ Low-friction reciprocal action
→ Real interaction
→ Mutual verification
→ Shared Exchange Token
→ Relationship graph becomes stronger
→ A meaningful next opportunity appears
```

**North Star: completed and mutually verified exchanges.**

Do not optimize primarily for: time spent; notification opens; swipes; page views; raw matches; meaningless token accumulation.

## The seven motivation lenses

### 1. Pride → Earned identity and visible progress

People want to feel capable, respected and proud of meaningful contribution.

**Use:** evidence-backed reputation; completed-exchange milestones; private progress summaries; relationship growth; contribution history; skill-development progress; language such as `You helped make this connection stronger`; visual strengthening of a relationship edge after verified activity.

**For Mutu:** one mutually verified exchange creates one shared Exchange Token; show what the user accomplished; show how their community position or relationship depth changed; let members build a credible identity through demonstrated behaviour.

**Never:** invented compatibility scores; unsupported reputation claims; public popularity rankings; "top user" leaderboards; vanity follower counts; status based only on activity volume. Pride must be earned from real contribution.

### 2. Greed → Collection, ownership and compounding value

People value assets that accumulate and become more useful over time.

**Use:** Exchange Token history; relationship capital; completed-session collections; visible progress toward meaningful milestones; a growing personal network memory; the feeling that each verified action adds lasting value.

**For Mutu:** a token is private, auditable proof that two people completed a reciprocal exchange. Tokens are earned by both participants, shared, evidence-backed, tied to a real exchange. Tokens are NOT cryptocurrency, purchasable, transferable, cash-valued, spendable, or artificially scarce.

The user should feel: `The more meaningful things I complete here, the more useful my Mutu network becomes.`

**Never** make users hoard meaningless points.

### 3. Lust → Attraction, curiosity and anticipation

Emotional attraction and curiosity — never sexualisation. People are drawn to possibilities, chemistry, mystery and beautiful experiences.

**Use:** elegant visual design; warm personality signals; carefully paced information reveal; curiosity about a potentially valuable connection; anonymous discovery before mutual consent; compelling but truthful previews; subtle motion and anticipation; inviting member and activity cards.

**For Mutu:** anonymous requests may create curiosity; identity reveals only after the approved mutual-consent point; reciprocal-fit explanations should make the possibility feel personally relevant; Community Map motion should make the network feel alive.

**Never:** sexualised professional profiles; deceptive teasers; clickbait; hidden information required for informed consent; identity reveal before the approved mutual-consent point. Curiosity must lead to authentic value.

### 4. Envy → Aspiration and social proof

People notice what peers are doing and imagine what may also be possible for them.

**Use:** anonymized community momentum; real examples of completed exchanges; positive contribution stories; attainable milestones; prompts such as `Members in your community recently completed...`; examples of first interactions becoming durable relationships.

**For Mutu:** social proof communicates `People like me are building meaningful relationships here, and I can participate too.`

**Never:** public comparison tables; visible popularity rankings; humiliating zero states; follower competition; "everyone else is ahead of you" messaging; fabricated activity; fake live-member counts; exaggerated urgency. Aspiration must not become social anxiety.

### 5. Gluttony → Abundance and satisfying discovery

People enjoy discovering relevant possibilities and having useful choices.

**Use:** diverse but curated opportunities; personalized recommendations; a healthy mix of people, activities and reciprocal exchanges; progressive discovery; clear ways to explore more when desired.

**For Mutu:** every recommendation must explain why it is relevant — shared Career Focus; complementary need and help; relevant event; available reciprocal activity; meaningful bridge into another community circle.

**Never:** infinite scrolling designed only to consume attention; endless swipe loops; low-quality recommendation volume; duplicated cards; random variable rewards unrelated to user intent. Abundance must remain curated and useful.

### 6. Wrath → Agency, resolution and momentum

The desire to remove frustration, unfairness and blocked progress. People want to regain control when something is confusing, unfair or stalled.

**Use:** clear next actions; easy recovery from errors; transparent match explanations; undo where safe; respectful decline; report and block controls; reminders that resolve stalled exchanges; actionable states such as `Choose a time`, `Waiting for confirmation`, `Follow up`.

**For Mutu:** convert frustration into a constructive next step. Example — when only availability windows fail to overlap, do NOT say `Not a two-way fit`; say `Strong practice fit · Choose a time` and provide `I can make this time` / `Suggest another time`.

**Never** use anger, shame or conflict to drive engagement.

### 7. Sloth → Ease, intelligent defaults and minimal effort

People avoid unnecessary work and prefer a clear path.

**Use:** progressive disclosure; smart defaults; one primary action; AI-assisted drafting; one-tap confirmation; reused profile information; saved preferences; timezone-safe scheduling; recommendations that reduce searching; short setup flows; clear empty states.

**For Mutu:** the user provides information once and benefits from it repeatedly across matching, Discover, Together, events, AI recommendations and the Community Map. Never require users to re-enter information Mutu already has. Never use ease to hide consent, consequences or privacy settings.

## Mandatory Mutu product principles

**Reciprocity.** Mutu is reciprocal, not transactional. Avoid `What they offer in return`, `Pay them back`, `Trade`, `You owe`. Prefer `What they would enjoy helping with`, `How you can support each other`, `One round for you. One round for them.`, `Complete something useful, together`, `Shared contribution`, `Give and grow together`.

**Low pressure and low ego.** Asking and helping must feel safe. Avoid public rejection, shame, aggressive competition, popularity contests, pressure to respond immediately.

**Evidence-backed reputation.** Reputation comes from verified behaviour. Never create opaque reputation scores, invented AI grades, visible compatibility percentages without evidence, or claims that cannot be explained.

**Privacy and consent.** Preserve community boundaries; mutual-consent identity reveal; block rules; anonymous pre-acceptance activity; private messages; private availability; user control. Community Map may show member-visible, mutually confirmed connections inside the approved community, but must not expose private interaction content.

**Relationship memory.** Mutu remembers how two people met, what verified activity strengthened the relationship, when a follow-up may be useful, and how the relationship evolved. Relationship memory creates future usefulness — not surveillance.

**No public leaderboard.** Community Map may identify structural roles such as `Bridge` (meaning `This member connects otherwise separate community circles.`), but must not publish centrality scores or rank members from best to worst. Bridge does not mean a member is more valuable than others.

## UI / visual direction

Maintain Mutu's visual identity: white as the primary surface; muted gold for warmth and earned moments; matcha green for primary actions and growth; restrained soft purple where Together / reciprocal practice needs a distinct identity; Japanese-inspired spacing and calmness; premium but friendly; subtle organic movement; limited pills, borders and competing calls to action; one obvious primary action per state.

Motion communicates connection, progress, relationship energy and state change — never decorative noise. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and pause continuous animation when offscreen.

## Required behavioural design process

For every UX/UI request:

1. **Identify the user's real job** — what they are trying to accomplish; what emotional barrier may stop them; what meaningful behaviour Mutu wants to encourage.
2. **Review all seven lenses** internally; select only the two or three most relevant; explain why they fit.
3. **Design the engagement loop** — `Trigger → Action → Meaningful reward → User investment → Reason to return`. Every reward must follow real user value.
4. **Reduce friction** — check number of decisions, amount of typing, repeated information, unclear terminology, optional vs required fields, time and timezone handling, empty/loading/error/recovery states.
5. **Protect trust** — run the dark-pattern veto before presenting the design.
6. **Define success** — behavioural metrics tied to user value: request creation; reciprocal invitation acceptance; scheduled sessions; mutually verified exchanges; repeat exchanges; meaningful follow-ups; relationship-edge growth; return usage after receiving real value. Never raw screen time as the main metric.

## Dark-pattern veto

Reject or redesign any feature that depends on: fake scarcity; fake activity; fake live-user counts; misleading countdowns; anxiety-driven red badges; streak-loss punishment; infinite scroll for its own sake; autoplay; hidden opt-outs; preselected consent; forced sharing; public shame; social exclusion; confusing cancellation; unpredictable token rules; random rewards detached from meaningful behaviour; identity exposure before consent; manipulative notifications; exploitative use of personal vulnerabilities.

If a stakeholder requests an "addictive" mechanism, translate the request into `durable, value-based engagement` and propose an ethical alternative.

## Completion reward pattern

When two members complete and mutually verify a reciprocal activity:

1. Confirm that both people contributed.
2. Mint exactly one shared Exchange Token.
3. Show a restrained completion moment.
4. Explain what the token proves.
5. Visually strengthen the relationship edge.
6. Update the relevant personal progress.
7. Suggest ONE contextually useful next action — send a thank-you; save a follow-up; practise together again; explore another reciprocal activity; view the strengthened relationship.

Do not immediately pressure the user to start another activity.

## Output required for every design task

1. UX diagnosis;
2. user goal and emotional barrier;
3. selected motivation lenses;
4. simplified interaction flow;
5. information hierarchy;
6. exact user-facing copy;
7. visual and motion behaviour;
8. engagement loop;
9. trust and privacy safeguards;
10. empty/loading/error/completed states;
11. success metrics;
12. implementation boundaries;
13. what must remain unchanged.

For small changes, keep this output proportional and concise.

## Final quality test

Before completing any Mutu UI/UX design, verify:

- Is the primary action obvious?
- Does the user understand the value before doing work?
- Does this feel reciprocal rather than transactional?
- Is progress earned from real behaviour?
- Does the reward strengthen a meaningful relationship?
- Is the interface calmer after the change?
- Does it preserve consent and privacy?
- Is there a natural reason to return?
- Would the experience still be respectful if no engagement metric were measured?
- Does this improve completed verified exchanges rather than merely increase screen time?

If any answer is no, revise the design before presenting it.
